## Introduction ✏️

> Tokolator is a **simple and user-friendly iOS calculator app** to **calculate transactions and manage item inventories for shop owners**. It is designed for easy calculations for any users despite any vision and dexterity issues. This 2-week project was developed by a team of six to accommodate the needs of **Emak Sri as our client** in this project.
> 

## Problem Statement 💡

<aside>
🧮

Emak Sri is a 65-year-old retiree who plans to open a small shop for leisure. She **wants to keep track of** **her income** and **item stocks** in her shop. However, it is **difficult to find a** **simple and accessible calculator** that accommodates her vision and dexterity issues.

</aside>

## Objectives 🎯

**Objective 1: Calculate Multi-Step Calculations Easily**

- KPI 1: Enable users to calculate transactions involving multiple items effortlessly.

**Objective 2: Track Monthly Income and Expenses**

- KPI 1: Record daily transaction details accurately.
- KPI 2: Provide a summary of total income and expenses for any selected month.

**Objective 3: Manage Shop Inventory Changes**

- KPI 1: Allow users to add new inventory items, edit stock quantities, and update prices easily.

## User Persona 🧑‍🦳

> **Emak Sri - A 65 years old part-time shop owner**
"I need to operate my shop as easily and efficiently as possible on a day-to-day basis.”
> 

**Behavioral Patterns**

- Uses a smartphone primarily for communication (WhatsApp), basic apps (Camera, Gallery), and internet browsing (Safari, YouTube).
- Prefers simple and easy-to-use interfaces due to potential vision and dexterity challenges.
- Often performs calculations for daily activities such as budgeting, medication management, and cooking recipes on paper.

**Pain Points**

- Finds traditional calculator apps difficult to use due to small buttons, small text, complex functions and less legibility when the battery running low.
- Difficult to find calculator that accommodates vision and dexterity issues.
- Struggles with remembering and performing multi-step calculations.
- Requires adaption to shift from traditional to digital calculator

**Goals**

- To access a simple and user-friendly calculator app (e.g. larger buttons, larger text, contrast,).
- To perform calculations easily despite of vision and dexterity issues.
- To have features that support one of their daily activities.
- To get assistance in using digital calculator.

## Key Features 🔧

**Transaction Calculator**

A **simple calculator featuring large item buttons** for easy selection. Users can add items to a transaction by selecting them, with options to undo the last added item or clear all selections. Once the transaction is complete, pressing the “=” button finalizes and records it.

## **User Requirements 👤**

- **As Emak Sri, I want to input add the items that the customer buys and record it as sales (High priority)**
    
    This user has a problem with using a traditional calculator and writing it on paper.
    
- **As Emak Sri, I want to know my history of past sales income and stock expense with their details (High priority)**
    
    This user wants to know the performance of each item to know the current sales trend.
    
- **As Emak Sri, I want to update my inventory after every restocks (Medium priority)**
    
    This user wants to keep track of the current stock of the shop’s items.
    
- **As Emak Sri, I want to know my monthly and accumulated profit (Low priority)**
    
    This user wants to know how the shop performs each month.

## Challenges  🚧

**Unclear Information Architecture**

- **Problem:** It was **challenging to clearly discuss the relationships between our features**, such as the transaction calculator, history, and inventory management, verbally.
- **Solution:** We decided to **create an Entity-Relationship Diagram** together to clarify the models and the relationships between the objects, ensuring a clear foundation for development.

**Challenging Accessible UI/UX Design**

- **Problem:** Determining **the right design approach for our transaction calculator** was particularly difficult because we needed to accommodate the vision and dexterity challenges of Emak Sri, our user. It was essential to make the items visible while ensuring that the calculation process was easy to perform.
- **Solution:** We **consulted with our mentors** for advice on the item buttons and undo button functionality in case the user made a misinput. Their valuable feedback helped guide us in improving the app’s design and accessibility.

## Future Roadmaps  📁

While the product has successfully met its objectives, there is significant potential for improvement in future iterations.The following are the top priorities for improvement, ranked by their importance to the product's functionality:

1. **Introduce a stepper for each item** to allow users greater flexibility in calculations, replacing reliance on a single undo button.
2. **Implement a deletion function for transaction histories** to correct any misinputs and ensure accurate records.
3. **Add a camera feature** that enables users to capture images of items directly when adding new entries to the inventory.

## Reflections 📝

As a product manager, I have:

- Gained a deeper appreciation for the importance of creating a **clear backlog with detailed acceptance criteria** to streamline discussions with developers and designers and facilitate efficient task division. This approach makes it easier to visualize workload distribution and track overall project progress.
- **Understood the importance of asking the right questions to the right person**. We relied on expert opinions rather than directly asking users about their preferences when deciding on UI and UX. This approach sometimes overlooks user needs, which can lead to an unsatisfactory experience. Listening to users is essential to ensure the app meets their expectations.
- Recognized **the critical role of user testing after completing the MVP** in evaluating the user experience, including the app's user flow and features. Due to the two-week timeline being heavily focused on development, we were unable to include user testing. In the future, I will ensure that testing is incorporated into the timeline when planning a product roadmap.
